Kostolany's strong and weak hands: who ends up holding what

Updated 2026-08-04

André Kostolany split market participants in two. The strong hands have money, conviction and patience; the weak hands buy because others are buying and sell because others are selling. Across a full cycle, he argued, shares move from the weak hands to the strong ones.

Why this maps onto a cycle

The observation is simple. Near a bottom nobody is interested, turnover is thin, and whoever is left has no reason to sell. Near a top turnover explodes and participation peaks — and most of the new arrivals are weak hands. That makes volume and participation the two axes for reading where a cycle stands.

Onto the six phases

  • A1 correction (up) — just off the bottom; thin volume, few participants
  • A2 accompanying (up) — the trend takes hold and participation spreads
  • A3 exaggeration (up) — volume explodes, participation peaks
  • B1 correction (down) — post-peak pullback; optimism lingers as strength fades
  • B2 accompanying (down) — the decline spreads and participants leave
  • B3 exaggeration (down) — fear and forced selling; participation bottoms

The catch

This framing is easy to apply after the fact. In hindsight it is obvious which stretch was A3; while you are inside it, telling A3 from A2 is hard, because a phase boundary is only fixed once the next turning point arrives.

So Kostolany Watch does not commit to one box. It shows a distribution across all six and states plainly that pinning the exact box has a structural ceiling. Kostolany's vocabulary is a memorable set of labels, not a timing tool.
