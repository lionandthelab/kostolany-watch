Telling a bull market from a bear market, and why the usual threshold misleads

Updated 2026-08-06

The usual shorthand: a fall of a certain size from the high makes a bear market, a rise of the same size off the low makes a bull. It is widely quoted and easy to remember. The trouble is that the threshold only settles in hindsight.

Highs and lows are decided later

To say “down from the high” you must know where the high was. But a high only becomes the high once nothing higher follows it. Whether today is the high is unknowable in principle; it is fixed only with the passage of time.

So “we have entered a bear market” always arrives late, and sometimes gets revised away. The threshold is not wrong so much as hard to use in real time.

The deeper problem: two boxes are too few

Bull and bear split the market very coarsely. A rise just off a bottom and a rise in a euphoric phase behave nothing alike, yet both are “a bull market”. That is exactly why Kostolany split the cycle into six — early and late look different even while rising.

Recast into something usable in real time

This site separates two questions:

  • Direction (the leg) — up leg or down leg. Decidable from price alone, and its historical hit rate can actually be measured.
  • Position within the leg — early, middle or late. This needs the leg's full length, so it is close to unknowable in real time.

The point is not to blend two questions of very different difficulty. Direction is reasonably answerable; the exact box carries a structural ceiling. That is why the two are shown as separate numbers.
