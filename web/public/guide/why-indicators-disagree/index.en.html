Why indicators contradict each other: RSI, MACD and moving averages

Updated 2026-08-09

RSI says overbought, the moving average says uptrend, MACD says the momentum is rolling over. Nothing is broken. They are answering different questions.

Indicators fall into three families

  • Trend (moving averages, MACD) — “is there a direction?” Good when direction is clear, noisy when it is not.
  • Oscillator (RSI, stochastics) — “has the recent move been excessive?” Good in ranges, wrong for long stretches inside strong trends.
  • Volatility (Bollinger bands, ATR) — “how much movement?” Says nothing about direction.

Trend and oscillator disagreeing is normal. RSI reading overbought in a strong advance is not an error; the honest answer to that indicator's question really is “yes, it has been a lot”.

Why adding more indicators does not help

The usual response is to add more. But most indicators are derived from the same price series. Watching five is closer to hearing two or three overlapping stories five times.

Worse, the more indicators on the chart, the easier it becomes to pick the one that agrees with you. That is confirmation bias wearing the clothes of analysis.

A less fragile approach

Instead of choosing an indicator, count measures that answer the same question. Ask one question — is this an up leg? — let several trend measures of different lengths each answer yes or no, and look at the tally.

Two things improve. The choice of which indicator to trust disappears, and disagreement itself becomes information: a day where the vote splits eight-nil differs from one splitting five-three, and that difference can be scored against history.
