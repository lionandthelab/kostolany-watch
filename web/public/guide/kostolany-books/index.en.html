Reading Kostolany's books: what to expect before you start

Updated 2026-08-15

Kostolany wrote a number of books, and Korean readers know them largely through a boxed set issued as the Kostolany investment series. Editions and titles vary with the printing, so this article makes no claim about any individual volume's contents or publication details and treats only the themes that run through all of them.

What the books are actually about

Emphasis differs from book to book, but three subjects keep returning.

  • Cycles — markets rise and fall repeatedly, and the rising and falling has a recurring shape. The egg drawing and the six phases come from here.
  • Psychology — what moves prices is mostly how people react. The split between strong and weak hands belongs to this strand.
  • Patience — money and ideas are not enough without the capacity to sit through time. It appears on every list he gives of what successful speculation requires.

All three are about perspective rather than procedure. Nowhere is there a table of rules of the form “when this condition holds, do that”. Knowing this before starting removes most of the disappointment.

Reading a memoir carefully

These books are largely recollection, and recollection has its own grammar.

First, it is a survivor's memory. Contemporaries who approached the market the same way and disappeared did not write memoirs. Reasoning backwards from one book to a method cannot distinguish a trait that caused the success from a trait equally common among those who failed. That is a problem with the sample, not with the author's honesty.

Second, memory smooths as it becomes narrative. Decisions that rested on thin reasoning acquire reasons in the retelling — not through dishonesty, but because knowing the outcome reorganises the past around it. It is the same bias that leaves only the flattering parameter combination in mind after a backtest.

Third, the era differs. Information speed, transaction costs and market access are not what they were. Whether an observation that held then holds now is a separate question, and the book does not answer it for you.

Fourth, an anecdote is a sample of one. The more striking the episode, the longer it stays in memory, and the more often it feels like something that happens frequently. That a story is interesting and that the situation recurs are separate facts.

On editions and translations

One practical note. The same original has appeared under different titles at different times, and the editing and selection vary between printings. Choosing by title alone can mean buying substantially overlapping material twice, so check the table of contents first. It is also why this article names no specific edition — not writing down bibliographic details we have not verified is the same rule the rest of the site runs on.

Why they are still worth reading

The value is not predictive power but vocabulary: they teach a way of naming market states.

Names matter because a named state can be recorded, and a record can be scored. “I felt uneasy then” cannot be checked afterwards; “at the time I read it as close to a euphoric phase” can. Vocabulary converts an impression into a sentence capable of being wrong — and only what can be wrong can be learned from.

There is a second reason. These books treat markets as a problem of patience and temperament rather than one of forecasting accuracy. That focus differs sharply from most current discussion, and the difference itself repays attention. What this repository measured points the same way: nothing was gained on calling direction months ahead, while describing the present state left a quantity that can actually be scored.

What happens when you try to test the sentences

Take a hypothesis out of one of these books and try to verify it, and three walls arrive in order. Knowing about them in advance saves effort.

  • What counts as the right answer. You must decide which stretch of data “the euphoric phase” actually was — and that stretch is normally determined by looking backwards over data that has already happened. The reference label consults the future, so a real-time call is scored under conditions the label never faced.
  • The sample is smaller than it looks. Daily observations are plentiful; counted in cycles they run to a few dozen. That is not enough to rank methods that resemble one another.
  • Eras get mixed. A longer window adds sample and drags in periods with different costs and market structure. A shorter one reduces that problem and shrinks the sample further. Either way there is a price.

None of this is the books' fault. But it is worth knowing how much distance sits between “the book says so, therefore it holds” and “we measured it, and here is what came out”.

What to do once you have finished

The most useful thing to do after closing the book is to write your own rule down. Three lines is enough.

  • What state do you read the market as being in — write the name.
  • On what grounds — write observations that can be checked later.
  • What would you have to see to drop that reading — write it in advance.

The third line is the one that matters. Move the criterion after seeing the outcome and no judgement is ever wrong, and a judgement that cannot be wrong teaches nothing. It is the same reason this repository registers its thresholds before running an experiment and does not move them when the result disappoints.

Then come back to the note later. The thing to check is not whether the call was right but whether the grounds were genuinely observable. A correct call resting on unobservable grounds does not reproduce next time.

Once that habit sticks, the books change function. Sentences you underlined become items that can be checked, and as the checks accumulate you learn for yourself which of them hold in your own market. That is exactly the part no author can do for you.
