What the fear and greed index is, and how to read it carefully

Updated 2026-08-04

A fear-and-greed index tries to compress market sentiment onto a single dial: low reads as fear, high as greed. Separate versions exist for equities and for crypto, built from different ingredients.

What goes into it

Providers differ, but the mix usually includes:

  • Volatility — how much the market is swinging
  • Price momentum — where recent action sits against a longer average
  • Breadth — whether more names are rising than falling
  • Safe-haven demand — money moving toward bonds or gold
  • Activity and attention — volume and search interest carry weight in crypto versions

Reading it carefully

The commonest misreading is treating an extreme as a contrarian trade signal. “Buy when there is fear” is memorable, but a fear regime can end in days or persist for months, and the index itself carries no information about when it ends.

The second caveat is that these gauges are mostly derived from price. Volatility, momentum and breadth all come out of price data, so the index largely repeats what price-based trend measures already say rather than adding an independent view.
